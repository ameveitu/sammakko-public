## Authored treatment and source comparison

Primary Stanford DREME material treats collection as serving a defined question/sample and includes all relevant variation. Our original garden question has a practical purpose (flower-colour map), a bounded picture and one record per flower. Children actually build a list from selected observations, can remove mistakes and check completeness/relevance. Identical red attributes from two different flowers remain two records. Second topic uses stones from the same area and starts a separate list. No count response is demanded.

Misconception example: collecting favourite red flowers misses yellow, including a watering can answers a different question. Transfer example: ask the same question of all members of an agreed group; a fictional play-preference survey illustrates one response per person. These are explained before practice. The module is image-based data collection under a supplied question, not independent question construction, an actual field observation or a real survey. It does not prove those broader abilities. Teacher-led offline pencil-colour collection offers real transfer; no personal records are requested or saved. Supplied words/colour descriptions are support, not naming demands. No original mascot drawings or source figures copied.
